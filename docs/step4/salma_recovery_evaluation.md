**Project:** LastMile AI — الإنذار اللي ما وصل  
**Owner:** Salma  
**Scope:** Independent audit of Agent 4's *actual* candidate evaluations; no edits to the existing agents, runtime, metrics, or datasets.

## 1. Evaluation basis and reproducibility

- **Scenario:** Fixed synthetic flood-warning scenario with 1,000 residents.
- **Reference snapshot:** `T+00:00`, with all five approved channels enabled (`CH01`–`CH05`).
- **Comparison:** Baseline `P0` and proposed candidates `P1`, `P2`, `P3` evaluated by `run_integrated_recovery` against the **same** stage, residents, network conditions, and channel assumptions.
- **Evidence:** Values below were generated by `analysis/step4_compare_plans.py` from the returned baseline and `tested_plans`, not manually estimated or inferred from the winning plan.
- **Limit:** These are *simulation results*, not observed deliveries, demonstrated human comprehension, or regulatory approval.

### Reproduce the comparison

From the project repository root:

```bash
python -m analysis.step4_compare_plans --stage T+00:00 --output step4_T00_comparison.md
python -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_step4_plan_comparison.py' -v
python -m unittest discover -s tests -q
```

The evaluation script also allows other approved stages (`T+02:00`, `T+04:00`, `T+06:00`) or channel subsets. Do not transfer the `T+00` numbers to other configurations without re-running them.

## 2. Verified P0–P3 comparison (T+00, five channels)

| Plan | Selected by Agent 4 | Estimated technical reach | Effective warning coverage | Change vs P0 (residents) | Unresolved technical critical cases | Selected attempts | Feasible |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| P0 | No | 961 | 0 | 0 | 39 | 1,000 | Yes |
| P1 | No | 939 | 0 | 0 | 61 | 1,000 | Yes |
| P2 | No | 934 | 0 | 0 | 66 | 1,000 | Yes |
| **P3** | **Yes** | **946** | **366** | **+366** | **54** | **1,000** | **Yes** |

**Key outcome:** The selected `P3` raises confirmed effective coverage from **0 to 366 of 1,000** simulated residents, a **36.6-percentage-point** gain. That does **not** mean all KPIs improved. Compared with `P0`, technical reach decreases by **15** (961 → 946), and unresolved technical critical cases increase by **15** (39 → 54). Both differences must be shown alongside the gain.

> **Important metric distinction:** Estimated technical reach includes delivered or delayed arrivals according to the project's metric implementation. Effective coverage requires supported evidence that the received warning is usable and actionable in the simulation. These measures answer different questions.

## 3. Agent 4 decision audit

**Selected recommendation: P3.** In the reference output, Agent 4 selected the feasible candidate that materially improved confirmed effective warning coverage, rather than automatically maximizing technical reach. The decision must be presented as a **trade-off**, not a universal performance gain.

- **P0 — Baseline:** Standard fixed-priority assignment, without accessibility-intelligent recovery. Useful reference point; technical reach 961, effective coverage 0.
- **P1 — Evaluated, not selected:** Technical reach 939; effective coverage 0; unresolved critical 61. Its tested outcome did not improve effective coverage over baseline.
- **P2 — Evaluated, not selected:** Technical reach 934; effective coverage 0; unresolved critical 66. Its tested outcome did not improve effective coverage over baseline.
- **P3 — Evaluated and selected:** Technical reach 946; effective coverage 366; unresolved critical 54. It improves evidenced usable warning coverage despite lower technical reach than P0.

All three candidates were marked feasible by the recorded candidate evaluations. The scenario-level candidate notes recorded **“No Issue Identified”** for Authorization and Privacy; that wording refers **only to checks within the synthetic scenario** and is not a legal assessment or authorization to operate an emergency alert system.

## 4. What the results cannot establish

1. **No evidence ≠ failed understanding.** `Effective warning coverage = 0` for P0–P2 means the simulator did not establish the required combination of usable, actionable delivery; it does not prove that zero people understood any message.
2. **Digital signage limits.** The model uses synthetic assumptions for signage placement, presence, and readability. It does not establish how real signs perform under field conditions or for every accessibility need.
3. **Critical cases remain unresolved.** Even in P3, **54 technical critical cases** remain. This is a technical-risk count in the modeled results; it should not be silently relabeled as a count of people unable to act.
4. **No universal optimization claim.** P3 improves the selected coverage objective but sacrifices some estimated reach and critical-case outcome versus baseline.
5. **No real-world compliance claim.** CAP, accessibility, privacy, and authorization discussions must be sourced and scoped to their documented use; no certification or approval follows from this run.
6. **Snapshot dependency.** These exact figures apply only to the reference `T+00:00` run with all five channels enabled.

## 5. Short explanation for judges (English)

Our Agent 4 chose P3 because it increased *evidenced, actionable and accessible* warning coverage from 0 to 366 out of 1,000 simulated residents. This comes with a trade-off: estimated technical reach fell from 961 to 946 and unresolved technical critical cases rose from 39 to 54. We expose those losses rather than hiding them. These are deterministic scenario findings, not proof of human understanding, field performance, or legal compliance.”
